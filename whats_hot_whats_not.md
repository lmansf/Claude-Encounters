# What's Hot, What's Not

As you gather your resource material, remember:

---

## People love to hear...

- What's important to the visitor
- Good stories
- Examples of things that we've come together and solved—e.g., the hole in the ozone layer
- Inspirational thoughts and quotes
- Surprising information in terms they understand—e.g., over 4,000,000 bathtubs full of water go over this waterfall every hour
- Things that evoke emotional or physiological responses—e.g., scary things, beautiful things, sad things, happy things
- Unusual facts—e.g., catfish have over 100,000 taste buds all over the surface of their body

---

## But don't really care much about...

- Ordinary scientific data—e.g., this waterfall averages 3,694,524 cubic feet per second in flow
- Doom and gloom predictions or rehashings of catastrophes—e.g., according to a Washington Post poll conducted in 2006, when asked on what year 9/11 took place, 30% of Americans were unable to answer the question correctly, even as few as five years after the attack
- The same thing they've heard or read at every other interpretive site or talk they've ever been to—e.g., 65 million years ago, this area was covered by a vast inland sea

---

> *"Interpreters are not teachers/entertaining fact givers—they are revealers/facilitators that provoke thought."*
> — Sam Ham
